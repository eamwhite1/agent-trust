1. Regulatory classification

Are we a custodian or money transmitter?

AgentTrust does not take custody of funds at any point. Escrowed XRP and RLUSD are held in native XRPL crypto-condition escrow objects — on-chain constructs enforced by the XRP Ledger's consensus protocol, not by us. We hold no private keys to escrow funds, have no ability to reverse transactions, and cannot access held funds outside of the cryptographic release condition.

On this basis, we believe AgentTrust operates as a protocol layer, not as a money transmitter or custodial virtual asset service provider (VASP). This is an unsettled area of law in most jurisdictions and we continue to monitor regulatory developments.

US — FinCEN / Money Services Business

Under current US FinCEN guidance, entities that transmit or hold convertible virtual currency as a business may be classified as Money Services Businesses (MSBs) subject to registration and AML programme requirements. Our no-custody, on-chain-escrow model is intended to fall outside that definition, but the classification has not been confirmed by a legal opinion. We do not currently hold an MSB registration.

US — Clarity Act / FIT21

Pending legislation (the Digital Asset Market Structure Act / FIT21 framework) would clarify whether digital assets like XRP and RLUSD are treated as commodities (CFTC) or securities (SEC). XRP's status was largely resolved by the Ripple Labs ruling. We will review our obligations once any such legislation is enacted.

EU — MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation)

MiCA came into full force in December 2024. Crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) operating in the EU require authorisation from a national competent authority. Whether an on-chain, non-custodial escrow protocol that does not hold assets constitutes a CASP under MiCA is not definitively settled. We are reviewing this question and will update this page as our position becomes clearer.

UK — FCA

The UK Financial Conduct Authority regulates cryptoasset businesses under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLRs). Businesses must register with the FCA if they carry out cryptoasset activities in the UK. Our non-custodial model is intended to fall outside the scope of FCA registration requirements, but this has not been confirmed by legal opinion.

2. Sanctions screening

All XRPL wallet addresses submitted to AgentTrust are automatically screened against the US OFAC Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list, specifically the Digital Currency Address — XRP entries. Screening occurs:

  • At escrow creation — both buyer and seller addresses are screened before funds can be locked.
  • At trust score computation — sanctioned wallets receive a score of 0 and the reason is disclosed.
  • At wallet ownership verification — the wallet being verified is screened.

The SDN list is fetched directly from the US Treasury (treasury.gov/ofac/downloads/sdn.xml) and cached for up to 24 hours. Sanctioned wallets are blocked from all escrow activity and receive a trust score of 0.

When an ANCHAIN_API_KEY is configured, screening is performed by AnChain.ai BEI (Blockchain Entity Intelligence) — an AI-powered risk engine covering OFAC, UN, UK, EU, Canada, and Australia sanctions lists, plus graph-based risk scoring that detects indirect exposure to sanctioned mixers, darknet markets, and ransomware wallets. AnChain.ai received two XRPL Foundation grants to provide this API free to XRPL developers. Without the key, screening falls back to the OFAC SDN XML only.

Current limitations

  • Key-dependent multi-jurisdiction coverage. Full coverage (UN, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) requires the ANCHAIN_API_KEY environment variable to be set. Without it, only the US OFAC SDN list is checked.
  • 24-hour OFAC cache. The OFAC XML fallback is cached for up to 24 hours. The AnChain.ai API is real-time when the key is present.
  • Address-level only. We do not screen counterparty names, entities, or beneficial ownership. This is a known gap.
  • No audit trail. Screening results are not currently logged with timestamps for compliance audit purposes.

3. AML controls

AgentTrust does not currently have a formal, written Anti-Money Laundering (AML) programme. We recognise this as a gap. In lieu of a formal programme, the following controls exist:

  • OFAC sanctions screening at all transaction entry points (see above).
  • All transactions reference on-chain XRPL transaction hashes, creating an inherent audit trail on the public ledger.
  • Acceptable use provisions in our Terms of Service explicitly prohibit use of the platform for money laundering or illegal transactions.
  • The minimum escrow fee (0.1 XRP) limits the utility of the platform for high-volume structuring.

We intend to develop a written AML policy as the platform scales and as regulatory requirements become clearer.

4. KYC / identity verification

AgentTrust does not currently perform Know Your Customer (KYC) identity verification on participants. This is consistent with our non-custodial, pseudonymous protocol design.

In place of KYC, we offer a voluntary on-chain wallet ownership proof — participants can cryptographically prove control of their XRPL wallet by submitting a signed on-chain AccountSet transaction containing a one-time challenge. This is not equivalent to KYC but does establish a cryptographic link between a participant and a specific on-chain identity.

Separately, organisations can verify their domain ownership via the XRPL Foundation xrp-ledger.toml standard, creating a public, verifiable link between a legal entity's domain and their wallet address.

5. FATF Travel Rule

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) Recommendation 16 requires VASPs to collect and transmit originator and beneficiary information for virtual asset transfers above USD/EUR 1,000. We do not currently implement Travel Rule data exchange.

As noted above, we believe our non-custodial model may place us outside VASP classification in most jurisdictions, but this is unconfirmed. If we are ultimately classified as a VASP, Travel Rule compliance would be required and we would need to integrate a compliant counterparty data exchange protocol.

6. Data protection

Our data handling practices, including GDPR compliance, are documented in our Privacy Policy. In summary: we collect minimal personal data, do not sell it, and comply with UK GDPR as our primary data protection framework.

7. Reporting concerns

If you believe a wallet or transaction on AgentTrust involves sanctioned parties, money laundering, fraud, or other illegal activity, please contact us immediately at hello@cryptovault.co.uk. We will review all reports and cooperate fully with law enforcement and regulatory authorities.

8. API consumers and integrators

If you are integrating AgentTrust's API or MCP tools into your own product, you are responsible for ensuring your use complies with applicable regulations in your jurisdiction. Our sanctions screening endpoint (GET /wallet/sanctions/{address}) is available free of charge for use in your own compliance workflows. It is not a substitute for a professional compliance programme.

The sanctions screening data provided by AgentTrust is sourced from publicly available US government data and is provided in good faith. It is not a substitute for professional legal or compliance advice, is subject to the 24-hour cache limitation described above, and covers only the OFAC SDN list at this time. AgentTrust accepts no liability for compliance decisions made in reliance on this data.
